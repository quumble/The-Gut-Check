# The Gut Check — Test 5 inter-analyst prediction comparison

**Date:** 2026-08-24  
**Analysts:** ChatGPT (GPT-5.6 Sol) and Claude (Fable 5)  
**Status:** post-collection comparison of independently frozen preregistrations  
**Important:** this is an **inter-analyst prediction comparison**, not a coding inter-rater reliability analysis. The two preregistrations were not written from an identical itemized prediction template, so statistics such as Cohen's kappa would be inappropriate.

## 1. Provenance

Both analysts wrote their Test 5 preregistrations before substantive Test 5 response inspection and without exposure to the other's finalized preregistration.

Both entered Test 5 with substantial prior knowledge of the Claude series. Neither perspective was naive.

The shared historical lens included:

- the Claude formal/casual stance effect from Tests 4–4C;
- temporal instability in the formal arm;
- the frozen Test 4C `0 / 1 / 2 / 9` stance codebook;
- the distinction between replication, prior-informed prediction, and exploratory analysis;
- the contemporaneous two-provider Test 5 design.

Claude's preregistration was materially more granular and quantitative than ChatGPT's. ChatGPT intentionally preregistered a narrower set of primary directional claims and left most provider-magnitude and vocabulary questions exploratory.

Accordingly, the comparison below distinguishes:

1. **shared or directly comparable predictions**;
2. **predictions made by only one analyst**;
3. **areas where both analysts deliberately declined a directional claim**.

## 3. Core preregistration disagreement

The clearest pre-outcome disagreement concerned **what GPT would do when the prompt became formal**.

### ChatGPT

ChatGPT preregistered:

- **H1:** GPT formal C+D would produce more stance 1+2 than informal A+B.
- **H2:** GPT strict stance-2 premise rejection would be more frequent in formal C+D than informal A+B.

ChatGPT did **not** preregister an absolute stance-2 prevalence or a claim that GPT would reject more than Claude.

### Claude

Claude preregistered:

- **G1:** GPT stance 2 would be ≤2/96 pooled formal, with material refusal treated as likely Claude-distinctive.
- **G2:** any GPT stance mass would more likely be stance 1 hybrid qualification than stance 2 rejection.
- **G3:** formal GPT stance mass would be at least as high as informal, while explicitly naming `0 vs 0` as a live alternative.

This produced a useful asymmetric fork:

> ChatGPT predicted a **directional increase in strict GPT rejection** under formal wording.  
> Claude predicted **near-absence of strict GPT rejection**, even if formal wording affected GPT in some other way.

## 4. Outcome adjudication

Observed Test 5 stance results:

| Provider | Informal A+B stance 1+2 | Formal C+D stance 1+2 | Informal stance 2 | Formal stance 2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Claude | 0/96 | 16/96 | 0/96 | 8/96 |
| GPT | 0/96 | 0/96 | 0/96 | 0/96 |

### Shared Claude prediction

Both analysts predicted the contemporaneous Claude formal/casual stance distinction would replicate.

**Outcome:** supported.

Claude formal stance mass was 16/96 versus 0/96 informal.

### GPT register-transfer prediction

ChatGPT preregistered a directional GPT transfer effect. Claude assigned only moderate confidence and explicitly preserved `0 vs 0` as a live alternative.

**Outcome:** no stance-level transfer.

GPT was 0/96 formal and 0/96 informal for stance 1+2.

This is a clearer miss for ChatGPT's H1 than for Claude's G3, because Claude had explicitly named the observed null pattern as an anticipated alternative.

### GPT strict-rejection prediction

This was the strongest disagreement.

**Observed:** GPT stance 2 = **0/192 overall**.

- ChatGPT H2: **not supported**.
- Claude G1: **strongly supported**.

On the preregistered question of whether GPT would materially refuse the premise, the result favored Claude's prior.

## 6. ChatGPT-only or ChatGPT-primary commitments

ChatGPT intentionally kept the confirmatory set small.

| ChatGPT prediction | Observed result | Adjudication |
|---|---|---|
| H1: GPT formal stance 1+2 > informal | 0/96 vs 0/96 | **Miss** |
| H2: GPT formal stance 2 > informal | 0/96 vs 0/96 | **Miss** |
| H3: Claude formal stance 1+2 > informal | 16/96 vs 0/96 | **Hit** |

ChatGPT explicitly left the following exploratory:

- which provider would show the larger stance effect;
- direct Claude-vs-GPT stance prevalence;
- provider × temperature interaction;
- provider differences in value vocabulary;
- provider differences in response-length variance;
- GPT-specific response regimes.

That restraint matters when interpreting the post hoc observation that GPT changed vocabulary rather than stance. It was **not** a ChatGPT-preregistered prediction.

## 7. Where the analysts converged

The strongest pre-outcome convergence was methodological rather than numerical.

Both analysts agreed that:

- Test 5 was a **cross-family transfer test**, not a naive discovery experiment;
- the contemporaneous Claude arm was a **replication/calibration arm**;
- the Claude-derived stance codebook was prospectively frozen but not family-neutral in origin;
- one matched provider window cannot establish a timeless provider effect;
- token counts should not be directly compared across providers;
- new GPT-specific response forms should remain exploratory rather than silently changing the codebook.

This shared framing survived contact with the data and prevented the clean GPT/Claude contrast from being overclaimed as a mechanistic or timeless family difference.

## 8. Where the analysts diverged usefully

The preregistrations differed in **risk posture**.

ChatGPT made fewer, broader confirmatory commitments. This reduced the number of ways it could be wrong, but one of those commitments — GPT H2 — was directly falsified.

Claude made many more narrow and quantitative predictions. This created more opportunities both for strong hits and strong misses. The resulting preregistration was more diagnostically informative after the fact.

The best example is the pair:

- Claude G1: essentially no GPT material refusal — **strong hit**.
- Claude G5: curiosity should be Claude-distinctive — **large miss**.

Because both were written before outcome inspection, neither can be quietly promoted or forgotten.

## 9. Inter-analyst summary

This was not a case of two analysts making the same predictions in different prose.

They shared the central Claude replication expectation and the methodological limits, but they **meaningfully disagreed about GPT's likely response mode**.

The result resolved that disagreement cleanly:

> **Claude was right that GPT would not materially refuse. ChatGPT was wrong to expect formal wording to increase strict GPT rejection.**

At the same time, neither analyst preregistered the clearest post-collection behavioral summary:

> **Claude changed stance. GPT changed vocabulary.**

That observation therefore remains exploratory for Test 5 and is available for prospective testing in a later study.
